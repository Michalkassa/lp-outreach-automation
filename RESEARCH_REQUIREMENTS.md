# Research Requirements — Valori Capital LP Outreach

## What we are looking for in an LP

Valori raises distressed credit and special situations backed by European real estate.
Tickets: €10M to €50M. Target net IRR: 15%+. First vintage, €400M target.

An ideal LP:
- Has AUM large enough to write a €10M+ check (generally >€300M in total assets)
- Allocates to private credit, real assets debt, or alternatives
- Makes investment decisions locally (not routed through a distant group HQ)
- Has a named contact who owns the private markets or alternatives allocation
- Operates under European regulation (Solvency II, IORP, local pension law)

---

## Scoring — 1 to 100

Scores reflect realistic fit: how likely is this LP to write a €10M+ ticket into a
first-vintage European distressed credit / real asset debt fund?

Be generous with partial credit. Unconfirmed-but-plausible facts based on entity type
or sector standard count — mark them [likely] rather than requiring hard evidence.
A subsidiary of a group with known alternatives exposure is a real prospect, not a dead end.

### AUM (0-20 points)
- >€1B confirmed invested assets:                                  18-20
- €500M-€1B confirmed, or parent group >€5B (subsidiary):         12-15
- €200-500M confirmed, or reasonable estimate for entity type:      6-10
- Too small or completely unknown:                                   0-3

### Alternatives / private credit exposure (0-20 points)
- Own confirmed local allocation to private credit or real asset debt: 18-20
- Alternative fund recently launched:                                  16-18
- Parent group confirmed + subsidiary plausibly participates:          10-14
- Sector standard for entity type (Austrian Pensionskassen):            8-12
- Actively preparing / stated intent to launch alt fund:                6-10
- Possible but no evidence; mandate type does not restrict:             3-6
- Restricted mandate (mandatory fund, 100% capital guarantee):          0-2

### Investment priority alignment (0-20 points)
- Named private debt / real asset debt / infrastructure debt in own words: 16-20
- General alternatives, private markets, or illiquid credit language:      8-14
- Long-term capital or yield focus with no specifics:                       4-7
- No evidence of any alternatives language:                                 0-3

### Operational fit — local decisions + contact (0-20 points)
Score both together:
- Fully independent entity, local investment decisions, direct named contact: 16-20
- Local investment committee within group + named contact with investment role: 12-15
- Subsidiary with some local discretion + named CEO/CFO with email:            7-11
- Fully HQ-dependent + no dedicated investment contact found:                   2-6

### Mandate and mandate fit (0-10 points)
- Mandate confirmed to allow illiquid alternatives:                 8-10
- Sector standard makes it highly likely (Solvency II large insurer,
  Pensionskasse, Czech 2024 reform-enabled pension society):        5-8
- Unclear, unverified:                                              2-4
- Restricted or prohibited by regulation:                           0-1

### Values / ESG / long-term hook (0-10 points)
- Specific ESG, sustainability, or long-term capital mandate in own words: 7-10
- General responsible investment or long-term language noted:              3-6
- None found:                                                              0-2

Total = 100 points maximum.

### Score interpretation
- 75-100: Top priority — draft immediately
- 55-74:  Strong prospect — research complete, draft when ready
- 35-54:  Medium — worth contacting, verify key gaps first
- 20-34:  Low — keep on radar, do not prioritise
- <20:    Skip — regulatory barrier or AUM too small

## What to find for each LP

### Company
- Legal type (Pensionskasse, insurer, Vorsorgekasse, pension society, etc.)
- Domicile and regulator
- Total AUM or invested assets (most recent published figure)
- Ownership structure (independent vs. subsidiary — note parent)
- Alternatives allocation % or private credit allocation % (their own words if possible)
- Investment strategy: how they describe their approach publicly
- Recent activity (last 18 months): new allocations, strategy shifts, public statements,
  fund launches, hires. Source everything.
  Searches: "{company} alternatives investment 2024 2025"
             "{company} private credit real estate debt annual report"
             "{company} strategy investment outlook CIO"

### People
- Primary: person who owns private markets / alternatives / credit allocation
  (CIO, Head of Private Markets, Head of Alternatives, Head of Fixed Income,
  CFO if small fund, CEO if independent small fund)
- Secondary: backup or investment committee member
- For each: why them (what their title/role tells us about decision access)
- Never guess email — use import data first; mark FIND with likely pattern

### Bridge hook
- One specific, sourced fact about the LP connecting to Valori's mandate
- Must be in their own vocabulary
- Strong bridge examples:
  - Named private debt as a strategic priority
  - Launched alternative fund (real assets, infrastructure debt, credit)
  - Yield gap vs. peers — their returns underperformed
  - Preference stated for managers with LP alignment or institutional roots
  - ESG / sustainability mandate that real asset debt supports
  - New alternatives hire or changed investment guidelines

## Red flags — deprioritise or skip

- Single-employer corporate fund with <€200M AUM
- Mandatory pension fund (Croatian Pillar II) — regulatory restrictions
- Entity being wound down, merged, or absorbed
- Vorsorgekasse — 100% capital guarantee blocks illiquid allocation
- No public data and no confirmed contact

Note: subsidiaries of groups with alternatives exposure are NOT red flags —
they score lower on local decisions but are still worth contacting and
may serve as a warm path to the parent.

## Segment-specific notes

### Austrian Pensionskassen
Best segment. Local decisions. Up to 30%+ in alternatives allowed by regulation.
Give +8 minimum on mandate fit without further evidence.
Lead with private debt track record and LP alignment.

### Austrian / CEE Insurers (Solvency II)
Strong segment. Focus on capital-efficient yield vs. public bonds.
Independent insurers (Grawe, Oberösterreichische) score higher than group subsidiaries.
Give +6 minimum on mandate fit for Solvency II entities >€500M assets.

### Czech pension societies
Changed significantly post-2024 — new legislation enables alternative funds.
Česká spořitelna and Generali already launched; others are preparing.
Do not apply old "conservative Czech pension" default — re-score based on current evidence.

### Croatian insurers (Solvency II)
Most are subsidiaries. Score operational fit lower but note parent as warm path.
Croatian subsidiaries of GRAWE, VIG score higher due to strong parent alternatives mandate.

### Croatian mandatory pension funds (Pillar II)
Cap score at 25/100. Mark as skip in output.csv unless AUM >€1B and alternatives confirmed.
Voluntary funds (Pillar III) have more flexibility but AUM is usually too small.
