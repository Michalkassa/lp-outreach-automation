# Research Requirements — Valori Capital LP Outreach

## What we are looking for

Valori raises distressed credit and special situations backed by European real estate.
Tickets: €10M to €50M. Target net IRR: 15%+. First vintage, €400M target.

**An ideal LP:**
- Has AUM large enough to write a €10M+ cheque (generally >€300M in total assets)
- Allocates to private credit — ideally credit backed by real estate or other hard
  assets (asset-backed lending)
- Uses the language of distressed debt, special situations, alternative lending,
  or opportunistic credit
- Is a pension fund headquartered in Europe — Central/Eastern Europe is the sweet spot
- Has demonstrated appetite for first-time / emerging managers
- Writes tickets in the €10–50M range
- Makes investment decisions locally — not routed through a distant group HQ
- Has a named contact who owns the private markets or alternatives allocation
- Operates under European regulation (Solvency II, IORP, local pension law)

---

## How to Score (1–100)

The score answers **one question**: how likely is this LP to write a €10M+ ticket
into a first-vintage European distressed credit / real asset debt fund?

**Be generous with partial credit.** Unconfirmed-but-plausible facts based on
entity type or sector standard count — mark them [likely] rather than requiring
hard evidence. A subsidiary of a group with known alternatives exposure is a real
prospect, not a dead end.

**One asymmetry to respect:** private credit missing from their website does NOT
mean they don't do private credit — many allocators disclose little. But if what
they DO disclose is private equity and/or real estate equity, that is evidence
their alternatives money already went elsewhere — penalise it.

---

## Scoring Layers — apply in order

Each layer carries a **weight label** that tells you how much it can shift the total.
Work through all eight layers, then do the sanity check at the bottom.

---

### Layer 1 — AUM / Cheque & Ticket Fit
### ★★★★★  CRITICAL — Can move the score by 20+ points

> Without enough assets, nothing else matters. This is the hardest gate.
> A €10–50M ticket is the target — score both the ability and the habit.

**AUM base:**
- **>€1B confirmed invested assets**
  → Strong foundation. Score can reach top tiers. No constraint here.

- **€500M–€1B confirmed, OR parent group >€5B with subsidiary plausibly participating**
  → Solid. Can write a qualified ticket. Note the basis (confirmed vs. [likely]).

- **€200–500M confirmed, OR reasonable estimate based on entity type**
  → Borderline. Flag it. Score is naturally constrained — rarely above 60.

- **<€200M or AUM completely unknown**
  → Significant drag on total score. Rarely above 30. If unknown, write [VERIFY]
    and score conservatively — do not assume.

**Ticket-size fit (on top of the AUM base):**
- **Concrete evidence of €10–50M tickets** (published fund commitment in that range,
  stated ticket policy) → SUPER MATCH. Strong additional bonus.
- **AUM in the €500M–€5B sweet spot**, where a €10–30M ticket is the natural unit
  → Mark [likely] ticket fit. Moderate additional bonus.
- **No evidence and AUM outside the sweet spot** → neutral. Do not deduct.

---

### Layer 2 — Alternatives / Private Credit Exposure
### ★★★★★  CRITICAL — Can move the score by 20+ points

> The strongest single predictor of fit. Do they already do this?
> Not doing alternatives at all is a top-tier negative signal.

**Positive tiers:**
- **Confirmed private credit backed by real estate / asset-backed lending**
  → SUPER MATCH. The maximum positive signal — they already buy exactly what
    Valori sells.

- **Own confirmed local allocation to private credit or real asset debt**
  → Very strong. Push score up strongly.

- **Alternative fund recently launched (last 18 months)**
  → Very strong. They are actively building this allocation right now.

- **Parent group confirmed + subsidiary plausibly participates** [likely]
  → Good signal. Warm path exists even if local authority is limited.

- **Sector standard for entity type** (Austrian Pensionskassen, Solvency II insurer > €500M)
  → Moderate positive. Industry norm supports it. Mark [likely].

- **Stated intent / actively preparing an alternatives fund**
  → Moderate. Directional signal — they are moving toward it, not there yet.

**Negative tiers:**
- **CONFIRMED zero alternatives** (reports show 100% bonds/listed equities,
  explicit no-alternatives policy)
  → Really bad. Big penalty — one of the strongest negatives in the rubric.

- **No evidence of alternatives found anywhere** (website, reports, news all silent)
  → Small penalty. Silence is a mild drag, not proof — thin disclosure is common.

- **Private equity and/or real estate EQUITY present, no confirmed private credit**
  → The single heaviest penalty in the rubric — heavier than confirmed zero
    alternatives and near-disqualifying. Their alternatives budget is
    demonstrably allocated to the wrong bucket. (Real estate DEBT is not this —
    that's the super match above.)

- **Private equity / real estate equity present ALONGSIDE confirmed private credit**
  → Small penalty only. The credit allocation is what matters; net the two.

- **Restricted mandate CONFIRMED in their own rules** (statutory 100% capital
  guarantee like Austrian Vorsorgekassen, or an explicit ban on illiquid /
  alternative holdings in their investment rules)
  → Strong negative. Hard blocker. Caps total score at ~20–25.
  Do NOT apply to Croatian Pillar II mandatory funds by default — they
  demonstrably make illiquid commitments (direct real estate, PE/VC LP
  interests); penalise only a restriction you can cite.

---

### Layer 3 — Strategy Language Alignment
### ★★★★  VERY IMPORTANT — Can move the score by 10–15 points

> Named asset classes carry far more weight than generic "alternatives" language.
  Look for THEIR exact words — that also tells you which bridge angle to use.

- **Named distressed debt / special situations / alternative lending /
  opportunistic credit in their own words**
  → Strongest tier. They think in exactly Valori's language.

- **Named private debt / real asset debt / infrastructure debt in their own words**
  → Very strong. Adjacent vocabulary, highest-confidence bridge.

- **General alternatives, private markets, or illiquid credit language**
  → Moderate positive. Right direction, less specific.

- **Long-term capital or yield focus with no specifics**
  → Slight positive. Directional only. Use as a bridge angle, not a firm signal.

- **No alternatives language found at all**
  → Handled in Layer 2 (small penalty there). No further deduction here.

---

### Layer 4 — First-Time Manager Appetite
### ★★★★  VERY IMPORTANT — Can move the score by 10–15 points

> Valori is a first vintage. An LP that backs first-time managers is one of the
  best signals available; one that explicitly refuses them is close to a blocker.
> **Explicit evidence only.** Do not infer from fund sizes or portfolio shape.
  Unknown = neutral, no adjustment.

- **Explicit evidence they back first-vintage / emerging managers**
  (a documented Fund I commitment, an emerging-manager programme, seeding
  activity, a stated policy or interview quote)
  → Amazing. One of the largest single bonuses in the rubric.

- **Nothing found either way**
  → Neutral. Zero adjustment. Most LPs never state this publicly.

- **Explicit established-managers-only policy** (stated track-record minimums,
  "no first-time funds" in manager-selection criteria or RFPs)
  → Bad. Significant penalty.

---

### Layer 5 — Geography & Entity Type
### ★★★  IMPORTANT — Can move the score by 8–12 points

> Where the LP is headquartered, and what it is.

**Geography (HQ location):**
- **HQ in Central/Eastern Europe, excluding Austria** (Czech Rep., Slovakia,
  Croatia, Poland, Hungary, Romania, Slovenia, Bulgaria, Baltics)
  → SUPER MATCH. Strong bonus.
- **HQ elsewhere in Europe (Austria counts here)**
  → Good match. Small bonus.
- **HQ outside Europe**
  → Penalty. Outside the target map.

**Entity type:**
- **Pension fund** (Pensionskasse, penzijní společnost, pension society, IORP)
  → Bonus. The core target type.
- **Insurer, commercial bank, promotional / development bank, DFI, sovereign
  vehicle, or any other institution with a balance sheet**
  → Positive-to-neutral. Any capitalised institution is a plausible LP — it has a
  treasury / proprietary book, a wealth arm, or a fund-investment programme that
  can allocate. **Never penalise an entity for its type.** Commercial banks are
  fine. (Leniency pass v4.)

---

### Layer 6 — Operational Fit (local decisions + named contact)
### ★★★★  VERY IMPORTANT — Can move the score by 10–15 points

> Can we reach the right person directly, and do they have local authority to commit?

- **Fully independent entity + local investment decisions + direct named contact**
  → Maximum. Fastest path. No HQ bottleneck. Score this tier at the top.

- **Local investment committee within group + named contact with investment role**
  → Strong. Authority is local even if group oversight exists at the board level.

- **Subsidiary with some discretion + named CEO/CFO with confirmed email**
  → Moderate. Contact exists, local authority unclear. Worth pursuing.

- **Fully HQ-dependent + no dedicated investment contact found**
  → Low operational fit. Treat as a slow warm-up path to the parent, not a skip.

> **Note:** A subsidiary with no local investment authority still scores positively here
  IF we have a named contact — that person is a relationship door to the parent.

---

### Layer 7 — Mandate / Regulatory Fit
### ★★★  IMPORTANT — Can move the score by 5–10 points

> Is their mandate actually allowed to hold illiquid alternatives?

- **Mandate confirmed to allow illiquid alternatives (via annual report or regulation)**
  → No obstacle. Positive.

- **Sector standard makes it highly likely** (Solvency II large insurer, Pensionskasse,
  Czech 2024-reform-enabled pension society)
  → Mark [likely]. Positive. No need to find explicit confirmation.

- **Unclear / unverified**
  → Neutral. Do not penalise unless you have found a specific restriction.

- **Restricted or prohibited — confirmed in their own rules** (statutory capital
  guarantee like Vorsorgekassen, development-finance geographic quotas, or an
  explicit prohibition; NOT Croatian Pillar II by default)
  → Under **Leniency pass v4 (full leniency)**: record the constraint in the flag
  but do **NOT** hard-cap. Treat it as a plausible relationship / vintage-II door
  and score on size, contact and geography like any other LP. A legal or mandate
  constraint is a caveat, not a disqualifier.

---

### Layer 8 — Values / ESG / Long-term Hook
### ★★  SLIGHT CONSIDERATION — Can move the score by 3–6 points

> Does their stated mandate align with Valori's long-duration, values-driven character?
  Rarely decisive — useful mainly as a bridge angle, not a scoring anchor.

- **Specific ESG, sustainability, or long-term capital mandate in their own words**
  → Small positive. Good bridge angle.

- **General responsible investment or long-term language**
  → Tiny positive. Note it and move on.

- **None found**
  → No impact. Do not deduct.

## Assembling the Score — the Heuristic

Use this step-by-step to arrive at a consistent number.

1. **Start at 50** (baseline: plausible LP, some evidence of fit, no red flags)
2. Apply each adjustment below. Combine them — do not cap after each step.

| Signal found | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| AUM >€1B confirmed | +10 to +15 |
| AUM €500M–€1B confirmed | +5 to +8 |
| AUM <€200M or unknown | −15 to −25 |
| Confirmed €10–50M ticket evidence (SUPER MATCH) | +10 to +12 |
| AUM in €500M–€5B ticket sweet spot [likely] | +5 to +8 |
| Private credit backed by RE / asset-backed (SUPER MATCH) | +18 to +22 |
| Private credit / real asset debt confirmed locally | +15 to +20 |
| Alternatives at group level only (subsidiary) | +8 to +12 |
| Sector-standard likelihood for entity type [likely] | +5 to +8 |
| CONFIRMED zero alternatives | −15 to −25 |
| No evidence of alternatives found | −3 to −5 |
| PE / RE equity present, no confirmed private credit | −35 to −40 |
| PE / RE equity alongside confirmed private credit | −3 to −5 |
| Named distressed / special situations / alternative lending / opportunistic credit | +10 to +12 |
| Named private debt / real asset debt in their own words | +8 to +10 |
| General alternatives / illiquid credit language | +4 to +6 |
| Explicit first-time / emerging manager evidence (AMAZING) | +12 to +15 |
| Explicit established-managers-only policy | −10 to −15 |
| HQ in CEE excl. Austria (SUPER MATCH) | +8 to +10 |
| HQ elsewhere in Europe (incl. Austria) | +3 to +5 |
| HQ outside Europe | −10 to −15 |
| Pension fund entity type | +5 to +8 |
| Fully local decisions + named direct contact | +8 to +12 |
| Subsidiary contact only, some local discretion | +3 to +6 |
| No investment contact found | −5 to −8 |
| Mandate restriction confirmed in their own rules (capital guarantee, explicit ban) | −20 to −30 |
| ESG / long-term hook present | +3 to +6 |

3. **Sanity check before finalising:**
   - Clamp the final result to 1–100.
   - Score >75 requires: confirmed alternatives AND local contact. If either is missing, cap at 74.
   - Score >90 requires at least one SUPER MATCH signal (CEE HQ, RE/asset-backed
     credit, explicit first-time-manager evidence, or confirmed €10–50M ticket fit).
     Otherwise cap at 89.
   - Score <25 requires: a hard blocker (AUM too small, mandate restriction, absorbed
     entity, confirmed zero alternatives, or PE/RE-equity-only alternatives).
   - PE/RE-equity-only alternatives (no credit anywhere, confirmed): cap at 34 —
     "Low" tier at best, no matter how strong the other layers are.
   - CONTACT GATE: no usable, current, right-desk contact (no email; contact confirmed
     stale; wrong desk/entity; PEC-only address) → cap at 45 until fixed. Record the
     ungated fit in the flag as "GATED(fit XX)"; fixing the contact restores XX via /rescore.
   - CLUSTER RULE: a subsidiary whose investment decisions demonstrably sit at a group
     HQ/asset manager (AIM, MEAG, Zurich, St. Gallen, Talanx, Trieste-framed carriers…)
     scores min(cluster-lead score − 20, 49) with a pointer flag to the lead row.
     Locally-governed vehicles (own board making allocation decisions) score standalone.

## Calibration v3 (2026-07-19) — Proof-of-Behavior First

Decided after the full 214-LP pass; overrides conflicting guidance above:

1. **Confirmed behavior dominates.** A confirmed illiquid fund commitment or published
   ticket (any private asset class) is the heaviest positive: +20 to +25. Halve all
   [likely]/sector-standard credits (max +3) and stated-intent credits (max +3).
   Stated capacity without action never outranks demonstrated action.
2. **Score = fit only; confidence lives in the flag.** The old "both AUM and
   alternatives unconfirmed → cap 55" rule is REMOVED. Every row's flag begins with
   a confidence label: [CONFIRMED] (AUM + key signals sourced), [LIKELY] (derived/
   partial), [UNVERIFIED] (VERIFY-based). Drafting order should require [CONFIRMED]
   or a verification pass — the score alone no longer encodes data quality.
3. **Cluster and contact-gate rules** as in the sanity checks above.

## Calibration v4 (2026-07-21) — Leniency pass

Decided by the user on 2026-07-21; **overrides all conflicting caps and penalties above.**
The prior framework was too harsh on non-pension institutions and mandate mismatches.

1. **Type never disqualifies.** Commercial banks, promotional / development banks,
   DFIs, sovereign vehicles and insurers are all valid LPs. Drop every "not a real
   allocator / wrong entity type" penalty.
2. **Full leniency on constraints.** Genuine legal / structural constraints
   (Vorsorgekasse capital guarantee, DFI emerging-market geographic quotas, etc.)
   are caveats noted in the flag, **not** hard caps. Score these entities on size,
   contact and geography like anyone else.
3. **No hard caps.** The sanity-check caps below (contact gate 45, PE/RE-equity 34,
   cluster min, Vorsorgekasse ~20-25, >75 and >90 gates) are **relaxed** — use them
   as soft guidance only. Clamp only to 1–100.
4. **Be generous with partial credit.** Silence is never penalised; "plausibly could
   invest" earns credit. Raise the floor; give the benefit of the doubt.
5. **Recalibration mapping (this pass).** Existing scores were lifted with a
   monotonic curve anchored so a mid-pack policy/type-mismatch case that read ~44
   under v3 now reads **65**, tapering at the top so already-strong LPs barely move
   (≈89 → 90). Ranking is preserved; facts in each file are unchanged. Future
   fresh research should score directly in this more lenient register.

## Calibration v5 (2026-07-21) — Tighten by ~7%

Decided by the user on 2026-07-21: the v4 register read a little too generous.
Pull leniency back by **5–8%** (≈7% applied).

1. **Scale down, don't re-derive.** Every existing score was multiplied by
   **0.93** and rounded (clamped 1–100). Ranking is preserved; facts unchanged.
   Example anchors: 90→84, 82→76, 65→60, 46→43.
2. **Future fresh research scores in this tighter register.** After assembling a
   score with the heuristic table and v4 leniency, apply the same ~7% haircut
   before writing it — i.e. aim ~5–8% lower than the pure v4 number.
3. **Everything else in v4 still holds** — type never disqualifies, constraints
   are caveats not caps, silence isn't penalised. This pass only lowers the
   overall level, it does not reinstate hard caps.

## Calibration v6 (2026-07-22) — Spread / raise the standard deviation

Decided by the user on 2026-07-22: the v4/v5 register was too compressed (most LPs
bunched at 55-70). Spread the researched scores so prioritisation is meaningful.

1. **Stretch around the mean.** Every existing score was mapped
   `score' = clamp(mean + 2.0 * (score - mean), 15, 97)`, rounded. Standard deviation
   rose from ~7.9 to ~15.4; ranking is preserved, facts unchanged.
2. **Limit:** LPs that shared an identical compressed score stay tied — a numeric
   stretch cannot separate genuinely equal scores without fresh research.
3. **Future fresh research scores directly in this wider register** (top real
   matches into the 80s-90s, weak or blocked into the 20s-30s), not the compressed band.

## What to Find for Each LP

### Purpose — research exists to do two things only
1. **Find the right contact — the CIO first.** Prioritise the CIO / Head of
   Investments / Head of Alternatives. Fall back to CFO, then CEO / board chair only
   if no investment lead is found. Identify who actually decides allocations, not just
   the figurehead, and record the desk, not only the title.
2. **Surface the one or two things the firm most values**, so the email can lean that
   way subtly. The draft never states these back to the LP — see voice.md.

Do not research beyond what those two goals need. No research papers. Stop early.

### Searches (~3 targeted — stop once you have a CIO + size band + one value thread)
1. `"{company}" CIO OR "head of investments" OR "chief investment officer" 2024 2025`
   — the contact, first priority
2. `"{company}" investment strategy AUM annual report 2025` — size band + how they invest
3. `"{company}" private credit alternatives real estate` — one value / priority thread

Run a 4th search only if the score is genuinely on a knife-edge (a specific mandate
limit, first-time-manager evidence). Otherwise stop.

### File depth target

Each completed LP file: **150–250 words** of substantive content (excluding headings).
Enough to draft from and to score — no more. Trim multi-candidate contacts and long
activity lists unless they change the score or the bridge. Specific facts over volume.

### What to extract

**Company website / annual report:**
- AUM with year and source
- Asset allocation (% alternatives, real estate, private credit) — note explicitly
  whether real estate is EQUITY or DEBT, and whether PE is present
- Their own words for the alternatives bucket (quoted) — watch for distressed debt,
  special situations, alternative lending, opportunistic credit
- Names of investment decision-makers
- Manager-selection criteria — any track-record minimum or first-time-fund stance

**LinkedIn (contact):**
- Career path: previous firms, how long in current role
- Any posts or statements about private markets or credit

**News / press:**
- Allocation changes or new mandates (with date) — note commitment SIZE when published
- Fund commitments to first-vintage or emerging managers
- Leadership moves
- Strategy statements (quoted, with source)

## Bridge Hook Requirements

The bridge must be:
- **Specific to THIS company** — not a generic sector observation
- **Verifiable** — something they said, published, or announced
- **Directly relevant** to Valori's mandate (distressed credit, European real estate, bilateral)
- **In their vocabulary** — use the same words they use for their alternatives bucket

**Strong bridge examples:**
- Named distressed debt, special situations, or opportunistic credit as a priority (cite document + year)
- Named private debt or real asset debt as a strategic priority (cite document + year)
- A past commitment to a first-vintage or emerging manager (cite it — this doubles as scoring evidence)
- Launched an alternative fund or mandate in the last 18 months (cite date)
- Yield gap vs. peers — their published returns underperformed comparable funds
- Stated preference for managers with LP alignment or institutional roots
- ESG or long-term capital mandate that real asset debt directly supports
- New hire for private markets or alternatives role (cite announcement date)
- Published target allocation % to alternatives they have not yet reached

## Accuracy Rules

- **Never invent AUM figures** — if not found publicly, write `[VERIFY — not found; check annual report]`
- **Never guess email addresses** — use import data first; for others write `FIND: pattern@domain`
- **Mark every unverified fact** `[VERIFY]` and note exactly what source would confirm it
- **If website or LinkedIn contradicts web search results**, use the direct source
- **Do not pad** with generic sector commentary — every sentence must be specific to this LP
- **First-time-manager appetite is explicit-evidence-only** — never infer it, never
  penalise silence

## Red Flags — deprioritise or skip

- Single-employer corporate fund with <€200M AUM
- Confirmed zero alternatives (100% bonds/listed equities)
- Alternatives bucket confirmed to be exclusively PE / real estate equity, no credit
- Explicit established-managers-only / no-first-time-funds policy
- Entity being wound down, merged, or absorbed
- Vorsorgekasse — 100% capital guarantee blocks illiquid allocation
- No public data and no confirmed contact

> **Subsidiaries of groups with alternatives exposure are NOT red flags.**
  They score lower on operational fit but remain worth contacting
  and may serve as a warm path to the parent.

> **Croatian Pillar II mandatory funds are NOT an automatic red flag.**
  Sector evidence (2025–26): Erste Plavi's mandatory funds bought a Koper
  shopping center outright; PBZ CO's funds hold PE/VC LP interests.
  Check each fund's actual investment limits instead of assuming a blocker.
