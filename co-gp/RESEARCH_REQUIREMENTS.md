# RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS — Co-GP programme

## What we are looking for

Valori is raising a €400M first vintage in distressed credit and special
situations backed by European real estate. €50M is committed. A Co-GP is a firm
that **manages the fund alongside us** and **brings capital with them** — the
match scheme asks for €100M, which is 25% of target.

That makes the question here fundamentally different from the LP question. An LP
is asked to allocate. A Co-GP is asked to partner: to put their name, their
distribution and their people next to ours for the life of the fund.

**An ideal Co-GP:**
- Can credibly raise **€100M** for a strategy like ours
- Has **distribution** we do not have: LP relationships, channels, a geography
- Can **manage together** — real credit or real-estate underwriting capability,
  not just a capital-raising function
- Is **complementary, not competing** — they do not run a rival European RE
  credit fund
- Has done a co-GP, seed or anchor arrangement before, so the structure is
  familiar
- Is an organisation we would be comfortable being publicly associated with

---

## How to score (1–100)

The score answers **one question**: how likely is this firm to become a Co-GP who
brings €100M and adds management strength Valori does not already have?

**Two asymmetries specific to this programme:**

1. **Capability without capital is not a Co-GP.** A brilliant operator who cannot
   raise is a hiring conversation or a deal partner, not a Co-GP. Score Layer 1
   honestly and let it cap the total.
2. **Capital without capability is an LP.** A firm that can write or raise
   €100M but brings no management strength belongs in the LP programme instead.
   Note it and move them rather than scoring them highly here.

Be generous with partial credit on everything else. Private firms disclose
little, and absence of evidence about a private partnership is not evidence of
absence.

---

## Scoring layers — apply in order

### Layer 1 — Fundraising capability
### ★★★★★  CRITICAL — can move the score by 25+ points

> The match scheme asks for €100M. If that is not plausible, nothing else in the
> rubric rescues the score.

- **Has raised €100M+ for a comparable strategy, evidenced** (a closed fund, a
  disclosed mandate, a public commitment) → SUPER MATCH. Maximum positive.
- **Raises institutional capital regularly at smaller scale** (€25–100M funds or
  club deals, repeatedly) → Strong. €100M is a stretch but not a fantasy.
- **Raises, but from retail, family-office or syndicate channels only**
  → Moderate. The money is real; the institutional path to €100M is unproven.
- **No evidence of third-party fundraising** (they invest their own balance
  sheet) → Heavy penalty. Cap the total around 35. Balance-sheet investors are
  LP prospects, not Co-GPs.

### Layer 2 — Distribution strength
### ★★★★★  CRITICAL — can move the score by 20+ points

> A Co-GP should reach LPs we cannot. Distribution that duplicates ours adds
> nothing to the raise.

- **Institutional LP relationships in a geography or channel Valori lacks**
  (Nordics, DACH insurers, Middle East, UK consultants) → SUPER MATCH.
- **Broad institutional relationships overlapping ours** → Strong, but note the
  overlap. Two firms calling the same pension funds is not distribution.
- **A placement or IR function of real size** → Good structural signal.
- **Distribution is one founder's personal network** → Moderate, and a key-person
  risk worth writing into the file.
- **No outward distribution capability** → Significant penalty.

### Layer 3 — Ability to manage together
### ★★★★  VERY IMPORTANT — can move the score by 15 points

> "Manage together" is the actual proposition. This layer asks whether they can.

- **Credit underwriting in European real estate, in-house** → SUPER MATCH. They
  can sit on the other side of a deal and add judgement.
- **Real-estate equity or development capability, no credit** → Strong.
  Complementary, and they read assets well, but the credit view stays ours.
- **Fund management capability in an adjacent private-markets strategy** → Good.
- **Distribution or advisory only, no investment function** → Moderate penalty.
  They can raise but cannot co-manage, so half the proposition is missing.

### Layer 4 — Partnership fit and alignment
### ★★★★  VERY IMPORTANT — can move the score by 15 points

> The literature on co-GP failure is consistent: deals collapse on character,
> conflict handling and misaligned philosophy far more than on economics.

- **Prior co-GP, seed, JV or anchor arrangement they are still in** → Strong
  positive. The structure is familiar and someone stayed married to them.
- **Complementary rather than competing** — they do NOT run a rival European
  real-estate credit fund → Required. A direct competitor is a hard blocker,
  cap at 25 regardless of everything else.
- **Stable ownership and leadership**, no recent break-up or litigation → Good.
- **Public evidence of a conflict, a partnership breakdown, or regulatory
  censure** → Heavy penalty. We would be publicly associated with them.
- **Values and conduct we would stand next to** → Mild positive, but a real one:
  Valori's own positioning is integrity-led.

### Layer 5 — Strategy overlap
### ★★★  IMPORTANT — can move the score by 10 points

- **Already invests in European real-estate-backed credit** → Strongest.
- **Adjacent private credit, special situations or distressed** → Very strong.
- **Real assets generally, or European mid-market lending** → Good.
- **A different asset class entirely** → Neutral to slight negative. Partnership
  is possible but the fund story gets harder to tell jointly.

### Layer 6 — Structural readiness
### ★★★  IMPORTANT — can move the score by 10 points

- **Regulated entity able to act as or alongside a manager** (AIFM, MiFID
  licence, or an established fund platform) → Strong. They can transact.
- **Uses a host-AIFM or third-party platform** → Fine, note it.
- **No regulatory permissions and no platform** → Penalty. The structure becomes
  a project before it becomes a partnership.
- **A named decision-maker who can actually agree this** — a founder, managing
  partner or head of the platform, not a business-development contact → Good.
  Without one, cap at 60: nobody reachable can say yes.

---

## Sanity checks — apply after totalling

- Clamp to 1–100.
- **Score above 75 requires** credible €100M fundraising (Layer 1 strong or
  better) **and** a named decision-maker. Otherwise cap at 74.
- **Score above 90 requires** at least one SUPER MATCH signal. Otherwise cap 89.
- **A direct competitor caps at 25.** So does a balance-sheet-only investor with
  no third-party raising.
- **Below 25 requires a hard blocker.** Otherwise floor at 25.
- If the firm scores well on capital but poorly on Layer 3, write in the summary:
  *"LP prospect, not a Co-GP"* and move them to the LP programme.

---

## What to capture per prospect

Fill `co-gp/prospects/<slug>.md` from `_TEMPLATE.md`. Beyond the standard fields:

- Funds raised, sizes and vintages
- Who their LPs are, by type and geography
- In-house investment capability vs pure distribution
- Any prior co-GP, seed, JV or anchor deal, and whether it survived
- Whether they compete with us
- Regulatory permissions and platform
- The decision-maker, and why them
