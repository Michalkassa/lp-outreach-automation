## Investment Strategy
APK's strategic asset allocation is approximately one-third equities (~31%), 50-60% fixed income, 5-10% property and private equity, and ~12% alternatives. Within equities, European equities account for ~8% and non-euro equities ~23% of total assets. Government bonds represent ~8% and corporate/other bonds ~35%. In 2022, APK tactically increased its alternatives allocation by 3 percentage points at the expense of equities and bonds, bringing alternatives to 12% of total portfolio. Alternatives are deployed indirectly, primarily through closed-end funds, across private equity, hedge funds, private debt, infrastructure, and real estate. APK views private equity as "a replacement for, or diversification of, public equity." The fund has publicly stated that "interesting opportunities will emerge in private debt in the next two to four years with restrictive lending conditions" and could set a new course in this segment. APK sees specific opportunities in the European real estate value-added segment, driven by refinancing pressures and a weak transaction market. The fund is cautious on senior private debt financing but recognises potential in open-ended fund structures. In 2025, APK shifted away from USD-denominated assets, finding Europe more attractive for sovereign bonds, European financial stocks, and investment-grade corporate bonds. No material changes to overall asset allocation were made in 2025. Target returns are not publicly stated [VERIFY].

## Summary
APK Pensionskasse is Austria's oldest and third-largest multi-employer pension fund with EUR 6.3B AUM. The fund has publicly and explicitly named private debt as its next strategic allocation, citing "restrictive lending conditions" as the opportunity, and sees specific value in European real estate value-added plays driven by refinancing pressures. With 12% already in alternatives deployed through closed-end funds, APK has the infrastructure and mandate to commit to new managers.
