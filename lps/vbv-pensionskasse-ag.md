## Investment Strategy
VBV runs a strategic asset allocation with equities and bonds at the core and has explicitly "expanded the investment universe to include private market investments such as real estate, infrastructure projects, and private debt investments" (vbv.at, Veranlagung). Private debt is their single largest alternatives bucket at 10–20% of pool assets, built since 2016 across 8–10 managers, and chair Günther Schiendl "enthusiastically endorses the private debt allocation," citing a stable 6% return through the pandemic and 2025 trade turbulence (top1000funds, May 2025). Real estate sits at ~5% and is "mostly European"; infrastructure equity at 7–8%. 2024 investment results ranged from 6% to 12.5% depending on risk strategy (VBV press, Jan 2025). In 2025 VBV introduced an opt-out lifecycle model letting young savers hold up to 60% equities — the fund is actively adding risk, not de-risking. Sustainability is integrated via Paris-aligned indices and every infrastructure manager must integrate ESG.

## People & Org
Decisions are made locally in Vienna. The investment departments of the Pensionskasse and Vorsorgekasse (€18B combined) are being merged, and chair/CEO Günther Schiendl leaves at the end of 2026 — Michaela Attermeyer now holds asset allocation on the executive board, with Martin Vörös on finance/risk (IPE, 2026). Andreas Zakostelsky is General Director of the VBV-Gruppe overall.
